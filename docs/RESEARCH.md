# Product research and priorities

Researched October 17–18, 2026. These are product-design findings and public-source observations, not interviews or evidence of adoption.

## Current event

The [2026 Neighborhood Hacks rubric](https://neighborhoodhacks.org/judging/) weights community relevance 25%, impact potential 20%, technical execution 20%, creativity 15%, presentation/clarity 10%, and accessibility 10%. The [rules](https://neighborhoodhacks.org/rules/) ask for a specific real problem and evidence, a working demo, disclosed tools, and a public code repository.

This event is in its hacking period as of the research date; no 2026 winner comparison is available. Earlier similarly named MLH events are separate competitions. Their results can inspire product choices, but must not be presented as winners of this current event.

## Local problem hypothesis

Residents may lose time navigating separate city, library, transit, conservation, business, and neighborhood channels. Public-source research confirms that these resources are published separately. It does **not** yet establish how often particular residents struggle or which task is most costly. User interviews and short usability sessions are still needed to validate that priority.

For this reason, every public factual entry links to a direct source, the app distinguishes member content from official information, and the project does not invent testimonials or impact statistics.

## Priorities implemented

1. **Get to the right action quickly:** unified search, task-oriented service directory, direct links and phone actions, compact navigation, and contextual guides.
2. **Make community interaction work:** shared storage, authenticated authorship, useful categories, replies, event capacity, expiry, resolution, and reporting.
3. **Reduce repeat effort:** synced bookmarks, reusable personal workspace, recurring reminders, route/time planning, calendar export, and portable backups.
4. **Show uncertainty honestly:** clear live-data failure states, timestamps, curated content dates, approximate map/travel labels, and no fabricated activity.
5. **Keep the interface readable:** restrained color, consistent hierarchy, accessible secondary-text contrast, keyboard focus, native modal behavior, reduced motion, mobile navigation, and larger text.

## Validation still needed from residents

Ask residents to complete three real tasks: find a maintenance-reporting channel, discover a free local afternoon, and offer or request a small piece of help. Record time to completion, where they hesitate, and whether they would return. Ask organizers which event/volunteer information they permit to be republished. Do not fabricate answers to these questions in the pitch.
